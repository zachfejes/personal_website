A needle, driven

A thin metal needle stands on a ground plane, fed at its base by an ideal AC voltage source. The needle behaves as a waveguide: current and charge settle into a standing wave along it, the wave leaks into the space around it as radiation, and the metal turns part of it into heat. Change the needle, the drive and the material, and watch the steady state follow.

03 — Heat

The current crowds into a skin a few micrometers deep, and that thin skin’s resistance turns ½|I|²R′ watts per meter into heat, most of it where the standing wave’s current peaks. In vacuum the heat can only leave by conduction down to the base, which sits on the ground plane at ambient temperature, and by thermal radiation from the surface.

05 — How it’s modeled

  1. Circuit. The needle stands on an infinite, perfectly conducting ground plane; the source drives the gap between the two through its feed impedance, with any base capacitance across the gap. By image theory, the fields above the plane are those of a symmetric dipole twice as long.
  2. Needle as a waveguide. The needle is a non-uniform transmission line against the plane (Schelkunoff’s monopole model): characteristic impedance Z₀(z) = (η/2π)(ln(2L/a(z)) − 1) from the local radius, so the point raises it. Series impedance per meter is jωL′ plus the metal’s internal impedance (skin-effect resistance and internal inductance, blended from the DC and thin-skin limits); shunt admittance is jωC′. The apex is an open end loaded by its hemispherical cap’s capacitance. The telegrapher’s equations are solved exactly in each cell (a graded mesh resolves the point) from the apex back to the source.
  3. Radiation. Energy radiated is a loss the line does not know about, so it is added as a uniform series resistance and iterated: solve the line, compute the power the current actually radiates from its far field, update the resistance, until they agree ({{ sol().convergence.iterations }} iterations here{{ r.converged ? '' : ', not fully converged' }}).
  4. Fields. Charge follows from current by continuity. The fields at each point sum the exact retarded fields of every current element and charge on the needle and its image, E = −jωA − ∇Φ and H = ∇×A/μ, using a thin-wire kernel that keeps them finite at the surface. Nothing is near- or far-field approximated, so the reactive near field, the induction zone and the radiation zone all appear.
  5. Heat. Time-averaged Joule heat ½|I|²R′ per meter. The steady temperature solves one-dimensional conduction along the needle (base held at ambient) with grey-body radiation εσ(T⁴ − T₀⁴) from the surface; vacuum means no convection. Material properties are held at their room-temperature values.
  6. Limits. The line model assumes the needle is thin (radius ≪ length and wavelength). Fields within a few radii of the surface and at the apex are approximate, the ground plane is perfect, and the medium is vacuum. Field emission and breakdown at very high tip fields are not modeled.
