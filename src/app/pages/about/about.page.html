The long version

I want to go to space. Becoming an astronaut is my primary professional goal, and close behind it is a broader one: helping humanity expand beyond Earth by building the infrastructure and capabilities that will get us there, and let us explore once we arrive.

Today I'm the Ground Segment Architect at MDA Space. The ground segment is everything on Earth that makes a space mission work, and my job is to design how it fits together: the systems and interfaces for mission operations and mission planning, the ground network infrastructure, the mission control and training facilities where operators and crew do their work, and the integration with our external partners. It's as much systems architecture as software, so much of my work happens where disciplines meet: guidance, navigation and control, mechanical, thermal, electrical and systems engineering.

I've been at MDA Space for more than five years. For most of that time I led the ground software architecture for Canadarm3, the robotic arm Canada designed for the Lunar Gateway, the international space station planned for lunar orbit. I owned the design of all the software mission operations would use to control it, from mission control on Earth and from crew aboard the station. Now I work across a range of programs bound for the lunar surface, cislunar space and Earth orbit.

I came to software by way of physics. I studied Electrical Engineering at the University of Toronto (BASc, 2014), specializing in electromagnetics, photonics and control systems, and I still love the physics underneath it all. I moved into software because code let me iterate on ideas faster than anything else.

I've also spent years in startups. Some succeeded, some didn't, and all of them taught me something. I was the technical co-founder of Sensassure, a medical-device company tackling adult incontinence and bed sores in hospitals and long-term care. I designed its custom sensors (from materials research to performance testing), the electronics and custom circuit boards, and the embedded C++; I also managed our material suppliers and drafted patents, and am one of the named inventors on our US patent (10,874,559). Next I founded Voyager, a software company for astrodynamics, spacecraft mission planning and visualization, built for space agencies, researchers and educators. It was my first deep dive into professional software design. Then, as technical co-founder of Second Closet, a storage and moving company, I built the website and web infrastructure, our customer and warehouse management tools and barcode printing, and pitched in on everything else, from running warehouses to investor relations. After that I joined RBC's Innovation Lab, first building voice apps and then leading its Voice Lab, exploring multi-modal interfaces with a focus on voice-first technology.

Away from work, I'm happiest learning something new and finding a way to use it. I draw, mostly in ink and pencil, and I'm fascinated by perspective and illusion. I model and design in Blender (which, admittedly, isn't really a CAD tool), and 3D print much of what I make. I play piano, read constantly, and am an unapologetic nerd. I'm also a strong believer in therapy, self-reflection and mindfulness as part of doing good work and living well.

I was born and raised in Toronto, and now live in Mississauga, Ontario.
